## Role in HMC

The Metropolis–Hastings algorithm converts an arbitrary proposal mechanism into a kernel that leaves the target invariant. HMC is a Metropolis–Hastings algorithm on an extended state space, with a proposal generated by a numerical Hamiltonian trajectory. The accept–reject step of HMC and its proof of validity are special cases of the material in this block.

## Required knowledge

- **Algorithm.** Given the current state $q$, draw a proposal $q'$ from a density $Q(q'\mid q)$. Accept $q'$ with probability

  $$a(q'\mid q) = \min\left(1,\ \frac{\pi(q')\,Q(q\mid q')}{\pi(q)\,Q(q'\mid q)}\right).$$

  If the proposal is rejected, the next state of the chain is $q$ again.
- **Normalizing constant.** The acceptance probability contains $\pi$ only as the ratio $\pi(q')/\pi(q)$, so $\tilde\pi$ may be used in place of $\pi$. In practice a uniform draw $u$ on $[0,1]$ is compared on the log scale: accept when $\log u \lt \log\tilde\pi(q') - \log\tilde\pi(q) + \log Q(q\mid q') - \log Q(q'\mid q)$.
- **Metropolis algorithm.** If the proposal is symmetric, $Q(q'\mid q) = Q(q\mid q')$, then $a(q'\mid q) = \min\big(1,\pi(q')/\pi(q)\big)$.
- **Proof of detailed balance.** For $q'\ne q$ the transition density is $T(q'\mid q) = Q(q'\mid q)\,a(q'\mid q)$. Therefore $\pi(q)\,T(q'\mid q) = \min\big(\pi(q)\,Q(q'\mid q),\ \pi(q')\,Q(q\mid q')\big)$. The right-hand side is symmetric under exchange of $q$ and $q'$. Hence $\pi(q)\,T(q'\mid q) = \pi(q')\,T(q\mid q')$, and $\pi$ is invariant by block [12](12-invariant-distribution-and-detailed-balance.md).
- **Conditions for convergence.** The proposal must be able to reach the whole support of the target (irreducibility). The possibility of rejection provides aperiodicity.
- **Efficiency.** The algorithm is valid for any proposal, and its efficiency depends entirely on the proposal. An efficient proposal moves far from the current state and is accepted with high probability. Random-walk proposals cannot satisfy both requirements in high dimension (block [15](15-random-walk-metropolis-and-its-scaling.md)); the Hamiltonian proposal is constructed to satisfy both (block [28](28-metropolis-correction-for-deterministic-proposals.md)).
- **Tuning.** Parameters of the proposal must not depend on the past states of the chain during sampling, since the kernel would then not be a fixed Markov kernel. They are adapted during warm-up only.
